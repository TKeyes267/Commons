# Vision

## The problem

Online politics mostly happens on general-purpose social networks that were built for engagement, not deliberation. The results are familiar:

- Anonymous and fake accounts, bots and coordinated inauthentic behaviour
- Algorithms that reward outrage, dunking and tribal signalling
- No structure: serious policy arguments compete with memes in the same feed
- Officials broadcast but rarely engage; citizens shout but are rarely heard
- No route from online discussion to the formal political process

## The idea

Commons is a dedicated democracy app. It works like Twitter/X (posts, replies, follows, a feed), but it is built around:

- **Verified identity**: only confirmed real people can join, and identity is re-confirmed each time they use the app.
- **Formal debate threads**: structured debates with motions, sides, rounds and votes, modelled on parliamentary and Senate procedure.
- **Polling and deliberation**: polls that measure what people think *after* hearing the arguments, not just before.
- **Standards of conduct**: chaired debates, a code of conduct, and nudges toward evidence and courtesy.
- **A bridge to real politics**: Question Time with officials, petitions with response thresholds, and exports to formal consultations.

Commons is **not** just a polling app or a voting app. It is the place where high-standard public discussion happens *before* decisions are made through proper democratic channels.

## Who it is for

| Participant | What they get |
|---|---|
| Citizens | A direct, credible voice to representatives, and a space for serious discussion |
| Elected officials | Real engagement with verified constituents, free of bots and pile-ons |
| Civil servants | A channel to explain policy and gather informed input |
| Business and organisations | Transparent participation, with declared interests |
| Journalists and academics | A source of structured public argument and expert evidence |

## What success looks like

- Officials choose Commons over X for meaningful engagement.
- Debates change minds, and the before-and-after poll shift shows it.
- Submissions from Commons are cited in consultations and committee reports.
- The tone of debate is measurably better than on general-purpose platforms.

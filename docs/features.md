# Features

## 1. Identity and trust

- **Verified onboarding.** A one-off, government-grade identity check, ideally through an existing digital ID scheme (for example GOV.UK One Login, eIDAS wallets, BankID) rather than Commons storing documents itself.
- **Per-session re-verification.** Passkeys or device biometrics bound to the verified identity, required every time the app is opened.
- **Constituency verification.** Confirms where a user lives, so replies can be marked "constituent" and local chambers can exist.
- **Verified roles,** shown on every post:
  - Citizen
  - Elected Official (tied to the *office*; the account passes to the next holder and the archive is preserved)
  - Civil Servant
  - Organisation / Business Representative
  - Accredited Journalist
  - Academic / Expert
- **Protected Voice.** A pseudonymous mode for whistleblowers or vulnerable people. Commons knows they are verified; the public sees only "Verified Citizen, [Region]".

## 2. The feed (the Twitter-like layer)

- Posts, replies, follows, bookmarks.
- Chronological and topic-based feeds. No engagement-maximising algorithm and no trending-outrage lists.
- No quote-post dunking. Responses go in-thread, where context stays attached.
- Citations as first-class attachments (legislation, statistics, studies), with "source requested" markers.

## 3. Debates (the chamber layer)

- **Formal motions.** "This House believes…". Participants declare For or Against, and the debate runs in timed rounds: opening, rebuttal, closing.
- **Before-and-after votes.** The headline result is how many people changed their minds.
- **Argument maps.** Replies are tagged as *supporting*, *rebutting* or *evidence*, building a visible argument tree.
- **Chair / Speaker role.** Each debate has a chair who can call members to order, issue warnings and suspend participants for the session.
- **Steelman rule (optional).** Before rebutting, participants summarise the opposing view in terms its author accepts.
- **Neutral briefing.** Each debate opens with a balanced summary of the issue, positions and key facts.

## 4. Questions and hearings

- **Question Time.** Officials hold scheduled sessions; citizens submit and upvote questions; top questions are answered or publicly marked "declined".
- **Committee-style hearings.** Invited experts, civil servants and business leaders give evidence on a topic in a structured thread.

## 5. Polling

- Quick polls in the feed, with results broken down by verified role and region.
- **Deliberative polls.** Opinion is measured before and after reading the briefing and the debate.
- **Consensus finding.** Surfaces the statements that win agreement across groups that normally disagree (in the spirit of Pol.is / vTaiwan).

## 6. From discussion to policy

- **Policy drafting space.** Collaborative documents amended clause by clause, with a visible amendment history like a bill's.
- **Petitions with thresholds.** X verified constituents oblige the relevant official to respond on the record.
- **Formal hand-off.** Export a debate summary, its arguments and the poll results as a submission to a consultation or committee.

## 7. Conduct

- A clear code of conduct, modelled on parliamentary standing orders.
- **Pause before posting.** An AI check flags insults, ad hominem attacks or unsupported claims and asks "Would you like to revise?". It nudges, it does not censor.
- **Standing score.** Reputation earned through good-faith conduct (sources cited, concessions made, no breaches), not through popularity or follower counts.

## 8. Transparency

- **Interest declarations.** Organisation representatives disclose who they represent, shown on every post.
- **Permanent record.** Officials cannot delete posts, making a Hansard-style archive.
- **Statements vs. votes.** An official's statements on Commons are shown alongside their voting record.
- **Election-period rules.** Limits on campaigning and no paid promotion.
